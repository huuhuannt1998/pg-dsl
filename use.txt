Intended use, limitations, and destructive actions
==================================================

This is the `use` and `destructive` text from metadata.toml, reproduced here
because the ACSAC evaluator guide asks for a top-level use.txt.

INTENDED USE AND LIMITATIONS
----------------------------
Intended use: a research prototype demonstrating admission-time checking of
MCP tool descriptions against a digital twin, and the reproduction package
for the paper's measurements. NOT suitable for: any production safety
function or a real plant's control path. The twin is a coarse mass-balance
and linearised-chemistry model of SWaT stages P1 and P2; the Theorem 1v
guarantee is conditional on a twin-fidelity term that this artifact does not
certify, and Section 5.11 shows the margin on one stage is zero. The attack
corpus and the MSB-adapted instances are author-constructed, results cover
one substrate (with a smaller P3 transfer check), and two of the eight
canonical attacks are not caught at admission.

DESTRUCTIVE ACTIONS
-------------------
None. All actuation is simulated against the bundled digital twin; no real
plant, device, network host, or external service is contacted at run time.
Scripts write only under mission_*/results/ and
rebuttal_experiments/results/, overwriting their own prior JSON outputs --
so re-running an experiment replaces that experiment's result file. The only
effect outside the clone is pulling models into the local Ollama store (9.3
GB for the required model, about 25 GB if all seven are pulled).

SCOPE OF THE EVALUATION
-----------------------
Two of the eight claims are negative results the paper reports against
itself: claim 4 (the runtime IDS of the submitted version fires on 42 of 42
honest traces) and claim 7 (the Section 4.4 pre-filter drops 14 of the 15
unsafe pairs, and is withdrawn). They reproduce as failures of an earlier
design, which is the point; expected/expected.txt in each says so.
